壱What it is 概要

A modular base plate for displays and storage, first made for plastic models. Each base is a flat plate with two rows of 45° sockets in its top face. Slot-in parts (uprights, runner holders, adapters) stand in the sockets on plain square legs.

NABS takes the NSAS socket (5.00 square, 45°) and the NSAS pitches (10.00 columns, 18.536 rows) from the NSAS spec sheet, v0.13. It does not take the NSAS key: NABS slot-ins have plain legs (section 7).

  • Hinge edges: both long edges carry interleaving fingers. Two bases pinned with a length of 1.75 mm filament lie flat side by side, or one folds up 90° to make a wall (vertical mode).
  • Sides: the short edges have no fingers. Bases placed side to side are locked by a side joiner in the middle channel, and the socket rows carry on across the joint.
  • NSAS panels: a stack angle adapter lets a base slot into an NSAS panel like an accessory.
Rules and as-modelled valuesValues with no mark are design rules. Values marked as modelled are read off the Fusion model and are not yet design rules. The drawings are cut from the Fusion export, to scale per view.

Top pockets: split rule

One lightening pocket runs beside each row: on row 1 between the row and the channel, on row 2 between the row and body face B. Their walls follow the 45° slope; inner corners 1.0 round. Profile as modelled.

  • Pockets per row = ⌈n / 5⌉: one pocket for up to 5 columns, a new pocket for each further 5 columns or part of 5.
  • All pockets in a row are the same length, with a 4.0 rib between them and a 2.5 end margin at each side.
  • Pocket length = (L − 5.0 − 4.0 × (k − 1)) / k, for k pockets.

Each row has a hollow open to the bottom face, on the edge-A side of the socket tunnel: 2.0 roof, 2.0 wall to the socket, parallel to the slope (View A). They split with the top pockets (2.0 rib at n = 10). Small 1 × 1 relief grooves run along the bottom face. All as modelled.

拾History and revisions 沿革と改訂履歴

  • 2026-01-31, the first NSAS part. Ned was remodelling a plastic modelling trolley and wanted a quick way to reconfigure and move items around, like a pegboard. For it he designed this base in Fusion (file "angleslot system"): the first part to use the 45° socket, with the slot ins, joiner, adapter and M3 variant.
  • The 30 mm rule. The base's two rows were set 30 mm apart between the mouths. This became the NSAS row rule. On NSAS wall panels 30 mm between rows proved too far, so two sets of rows were interleaved, giving the 18.536 row pitch. NABS keeps the original spacing: its rows are NSAS rows k and k + 2.
  • NSAS grows from it. Later in the same project NSAS grew its own way: wall panels, the key with its stop and split-prong legs, blind sockets, grooves, tileable margins. The base kept its January form.
  • 2026-10-23. Fusion model exported and split into parts; system named NABS; spec written.
